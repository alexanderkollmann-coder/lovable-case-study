# Measurement

> If we cannot tie hackathon activity to revenue, the program is brand theatre. We can. Here is the scorecard.

## North star

**Hackathon-sourced ARR.** Every hackathon-touched account gets a tag in Salesforce. Net new ARR + expansion ARR from those accounts is the headline metric.

12-month target (assuming 12 1:1 hackathons in year 1):

> **£3M–£6M sourced ARR + £8M–£15M influenced expansion**, hackathon attribution windowed at 12 months from the day the MOU is signed.

## Leading indicators — measured weekly

These tell us the *funnel* is healthy long before ARR lands.

| Metric | Definition | Target | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MOU same-day rate** | % of 1:1 hackathons that produce a signed Optionsvereinbarung within 24h | ≥80% | Day-of execution health |
| **POC kickoff rate** | % of MOUs that initiate a paid POC within 30 days | ≥75% | Post-event momentum |
| **POC-to-SoW rate** | % of POCs that convert to a signed SoW within 90 days | ≥60% | The hard one. The deal-making layer. |
| **Active Lovable seats / hackathon attendee** | Builders still active 30 days post-event | ≥70% | Tells us the platform is sticky, not novelty |
| **Hackathon NPS** | From attendees + sponsor | ≥50 | Brand health + word-of-mouth |

## Lagging indicators — measured quarterly

These confirm the leading indicators are translating to revenue.

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Hackathon-sourced ARR** | New + expansion ARR from hackathon-tagged accounts (the north star) |
| **NDR uplift** | Hackathon-attended accounts vs. matched control cohort. Target: +25 percentage points. |
| **Time-to-revenue** | Median days from hackathon → first SoW invoice. Target: ≤120 days. |
| **Reference contributions** | # of hackathon customers who agreed to a public case study or speaker slot |
| **Customer expansion ARR** | Existing accounts that ran a hackathon → expansion ARR in the next 12 months |

## Brand & flywheel metrics

The "soft" numbers that matter for category positioning.

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Press mentions** (FT, Sifted, TechCrunch, vertical trades) | Shapes EMEA narrative ahead of v0's London push |
| **Inbound hackathon requests** | Demand signal — when prospects ask *us* for a hackathon, the flywheel is working |
| **Hackathon Hall of Fame engagement** (forks, views) | Direct measure of post-event distribution |
| **Recruiting pipeline contribution** | # hires sourced from hackathon attendees + judges + builders |

## What I will *not* measure

- **Builder satisfaction surveys without NPS context** — vanity score, easily gamed
- **Number of prototypes built** — quality matters, count doesn't
- **Twitter impressions** — not a revenue metric
- **Hours saved per prototype** — speculative, customer self-reports it; not real

## Leading vs. lagging — the discipline

| Phase | What I steer on |
|---|---|
| **Day 1–30 of role** | MOU same-day rate (binary day-of execution check) |
| **Quarter 1** | POC kickoff rate, POC-to-SoW rate (do the deals start?) |
| **Quarter 2–4** | Hackathon-sourced ARR, NDR uplift (do the deals close, expand?) |

Anyone who steers on hackathon-sourced ARR in Quarter 1 is steering on noise. The funnel takes 90 days to fill. The discipline is **leading metrics until the lagging ones are statistically meaningful.**

## How I report this to the exec team

A monthly hackathon dashboard, deployed *on Lovable*, with:

1. **North-star ARR card** — sourced + influenced, 12-month rolling
2. **Funnel chart** — hackathons run → MOUs → POCs → SoWs → live deployments
3. **Per-account drill-down** — click an account to see its full journey
4. **Cohort comparison** — hackathon-attended vs. matched-control NDR over 12 months
5. **Leading-indicator strip** — MOU same-day rate, POC kickoff rate, weekly trends
6. **Hall of Fame engagement** summary
7. **Forward calendar** — next 8 weeks, confidence-rated pipeline

Every other slide in any business review goes through this dashboard.

## Benchmark against peers

- **HubSpot** measures partner-event-to-pipeline conversion at ~30% MOU → SoW. We should beat this — Lovable's hackathon experience is more visceral than a HubSpot Solutions Partner workshop.
- **Klaviyo** measured Shopify-event-to-pipeline at 25%+ at 12-month conversion. Hackathons should be 2–3× because the format converts faster.
- **Notion** measures community event NPS but not pipeline rigorously — we should run a tighter loop than they do.

## Open question for the panel

> The 65% POC-to-SoW conversion target is aggressive. If we land at 50% in Quarter 1, do we double down (it's a learning curve) or pivot the format mix (Embedded Sprint instead of Team Build)? My instinct: double down, because Q1 sample is too small to trust.
