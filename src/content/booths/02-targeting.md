# Targeting

> How we got to Allianz Partners — and the framework that lets us repeat it.

## The ICP, in one sentence

> **Mid-to-large enterprises with an AI mandate already on the executive agenda, regulated complexity that requires governance Lovable provides, and a procurement function that has not yet decided between Lovable and v0.**

## The three-axis qualification

Each axis 1–5; we run hackathons against accounts that score in the top quartile across all three.

| Axis | Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **NDR potential** | Active Lovable usage in pockets, request volume, internal-tool TAM | Hackathon ROI lives in expansion, not net-new |
| **Strategic narrative fit** | Public AI roadmap, regulated complexity, "innovation theatre" appetite | These accounts publicly endorse winners — the reference flywheel |
| **Procurement viability** | SOC 2 cleared, MSA signed or active vendor onboarding | Hackathon→POC stalls when security re-runs from zero |

## Prioritization tiers

**Tier 1 — Anchor accounts** *(this is where Allianz Partners sits)*
- Already in active conversation
- Existing Lovable footprint somewhere in the org
- Named exec sponsor we can reach
- Vertical-defining (a "yes" unlocks 5–10 sector-adjacent peers)

**Tier 2 — Reference-flywheel accounts**
- High narrative fit; less immediate footprint
- Unblock once Tier 1 ship a case study

**Tier 3 — Inbound qualified**
- Self-served into Lovable, asking *us* for a hackathon
- Run a leaner format, lower cost to serve

## How we got to Allianz Partners specifically

A four-step sequence — the same structure I'd use for the next 20 accounts:

1. **Map the AI roadmap publicly** — Allianz Partners had announced AI initiatives across claims, underwriting, and broker tooling. Their CEO had committed to an AI-native operating model in the 2025 annual letter. The mandate was already live; we did not have to create urgency.

2. **Identify the operational pain** — claims triage and underwriting workbenches were named in their roadmap as the highest-volume, lowest-velocity workflows. Those are *exactly* the use cases Lovable's prototype-to-production motion compresses by 80%.

3. **Get the right three people in the same room** — see stakeholder map below.

4. **Lead with the hackathon, not the demo** — the first proposal to them was *not* "let us walk you through Lovable." It was "give us your three hardest internal-tool problems and let your own people build the answer in a day." That reframed the conversation.

## The stakeholder map — three roles, always

A hackathon proposal that lands needs **all three** of these in the room. Miss one and the deal stalls.

| Role | Who | What they need from us |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical sponsor** | CIO / CTO / Chief Architect | Confidence Lovable will pass procurement: SOC2, audit trails, data residency, model-training opt-out |
| **Business sponsor** | LOB head — Chief Underwriter, Head of Claims, Head of Digital | Proof the prototypes will land in *their* P&L. Specific problem statements they own. |
| **Executive sponsor** | Chief Innovation Officer / CDO / Chief Strategy Officer | A public win they can attach to the AI roadmap they already announced. |

For Allianz Partners specifically:
- Technical: Group CIO + the regional Head of Architecture
- Business: Chief Claims Officer + Head of Broker Solutions
- Exec: Chief Innovation Officer (the AI mandate sits with them)

## What I will *not* target

- Pure SaaS-native scaleups — they self-serve into Lovable; hackathon is overkill
- Heavily-regulated public sector before Lovable's SOC 2 Type II ships
- Accounts where v0 is already procured at the platform level — that is competitive *displacement*, a different motion

## Generalizable, not bespoke

The four-step sequence above is the **playbook**. Every name in this document changes for the next account. The structure does not.
