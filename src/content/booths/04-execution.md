# Execution

> The day, beat by beat. Walk into the room with this playbook in hand.

## The five-act structure

The hackathon day is a five-act narrative — each act has a deliverable, an owner, and a cliffhanger that pushes into the next.

```
ACT 1 ─ Business case definition       (45 min)   "What problem are we solving?"
ACT 2 ─ Problem solving                 (60 min)   "What does the answer look like?"
ACT 3 ─ Build                          (4–5 hrs)   "Make it work."
ACT 4 ─ Demo                            (40 min)   "Show what shipped."
ACT 5 ─ Award + commitment              (30 min)   "Lock the next step."
```

## Run-of-show

| Time | Block | Owner | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **09:00** | Welcome + framing | Customer exec sponsor | "Why this matters today" — sets stakes |
| **09:15** | Lovable demo + AI literacy primer | Lovable Solutions Engineer | Shared baseline — everyone has seen the product work |
| **09:35** | **Business case definition** *(Act 1)* | Strategic Initiatives + customer SMEs | Three problem statements, written, signed off by LOB sponsors |
| **10:20** | **Problem solving** *(Act 2)* | Mixed teams (4 builders each) | Solution architecture sketch per team |
| **11:20** | **Sprint 1 — build** | Builders | First working prototype |
| **13:00** | Lunch + builder lightning show-and-tells (60 min) | All | Cross-pollination |
| **14:00** | **Sprint 2 — refine + demo prep** | Builders | Polished demo, scripted handoff narrative |
| **16:30** | **Demos** *(Act 4)* | Builders, 5 min each | 5–8 working applications, ready for handoff |
| **17:10** | Judging deliberation | Panel | Top-1 + honourable mentions |
| **17:30** | **Award + executive commitment** *(Act 5)* | Customer exec + Lovable founder | Signed MOU on the winning idea |
| **18:00** | Cocktails + 1-on-1 follow-through booking | Strategic Initiatives + AE | Next-meeting calendar holds |

## Who's in the room

**On the customer side**:
- 20–40 builders (engineers, PMs, designers, ops, marketing — *not* just devs)
- 2–3 LOB sponsors who own the problem statements
- 1 exec sponsor — present at Welcome, Demos, Award (mandatory)

**On the Lovable side**:
- **Strategic Initiatives Lead** *(my role on the day)* — runs the floor, owns the run-of-show, owns the MOU at 17:30
- **1 Solutions Engineer** — technical backstop during build, ensures Lovable does not fail under load
- **1 AE** — relationship management, handles next-step calendar holds
- **1 founder/CEO** *(virtual or in-person)* — closes Act 5, gravitas signal

**On the partner side**:
- 1 foundation-model partner rep (Anthropic / OpenAI / Google) on judging panel — credibility multiplier
- Optional: 1 vertical specialist (e.g. for Allianz Partners, an InsureTech analyst)

## Deliverables at each block

| Act | Hard artifact produced |
|---|---|
| 1. Business case | Three written problem statements + acceptance criteria |
| 2. Problem solving | One solution-architecture sketch per team |
| 3. Build | 5–8 working Lovable apps, deployed to staging URLs the customer owns |
| 4. Demo | Recorded demos + a written 1-page summary per team |
| 5. Award | **Signed Optionsvereinbarung (MOU) on the winning idea** — locks 30-day paid POC, no procurement re-runs |

## The most under-invested 90 minutes

**Act 1 — Business Case Definition.**

Hackathons that fail almost always fail because the problem statements were vague. "Help us automate claims" is not a problem statement. "Reduce average claims-triage time from 40 minutes to under 5 minutes for low-complexity motor claims, with auditable decision trails" *is* a problem statement.

This block is co-designed at T-14 (two weeks before the event), refined at T-3, and signed off at the start of the day. **Skipping the co-design is the single highest-correlation predictor of a weak hackathon outcome.**

## Failure modes and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Exec sponsor cancels day-of | Pre-confirmed at T-7. Day-of cameo locked in writing. |
| Builders too junior, output weak | Pre-screen attendee list at T-21 with the customer champion |
| MOU not pre-cleared by legal | Standard template signed off in MSA addendum at T-30 |
| One team's prototype crashes during demo | SE has a hot-spare deploy ready; demo proceeds with backup |
| Customer wants to "extend the hackathon by 3 hours" | Politely decline. Energy management beats output volume. |

## What I personally run

I'm on the floor 09:00–18:00. I am the **only person** holding the run-of-show end-to-end. I do not present, I do not judge — I orchestrate.

Specifically:
- I keep blocks on time (a 15-minute slip in Act 1 wrecks the rest of the day)
- I hand the mic between speakers to maintain narrative flow
- I sit beside the exec sponsor at lunch to pre-sell the MOU before Act 5 starts
- I read the room — if a team is stuck, the SE goes to them; if the exec is bored, I move them to a builder station
- I close the day with the MOU signing, scheduled to land while the energy is highest

This is closer to **directing a film** than running a meeting. The choreography is the deliverable.
