# Scale

> 1:1 → 1:Few → 1:Many. Each step trades depth for breadth — but the ratio doesn't have to collapse.

## The ladder

| Stage | Format | ARR per event | Touch | Time-to-deploy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1:1** | Single-account exec or team build | £100K–500K (POC + SoW) | High-touch | 90 days |
| **1:Few** | Sector cohort (4 customers) | £300K–800K | Medium-touch | 90–120 days |
| **1:Many** | Industry flagship (open invite) | £100K–300K (mostly indirect, brand-led) | Low-touch | 180+ days |

## How impact actually shifts

The naïve assumption is that **breadth dilutes depth**. It doesn't have to — *if* the playbook is templatized.

**1:1 generates the patterns. 1:Few stress-tests them. 1:Many distributes them.**

Every 1:1 produces 2–4 reusable artefacts (problem-statement template, run-of-show, demo brief, MOU language). After ten 1:1s, the 1:Few format runs at 80% of the 1:1 quality at 30% of the marginal cost. After three 1:Few events, the 1:Many format produces brand at near-zero incremental Lovable cost.

## The Applications group — the scale flywheel inside Lovable

This is the engine that makes 1:Few and 1:Many actually work.

A small team (initially 2 people) inside Strategic Initiatives owns:

- **Reference Implementation Library** — 10–15 forkable Lovable apps for regulated verticals, each with auth, RBAC, audit, governance baked in
- **Sector packs** — vertical-specific bundles (Insurance, Banking, Pharma, Retail) including problem-statement libraries, sample data shapes, judging rubrics
- **POC accelerators** — pre-cleared connectors, security questionnaire pre-fills, DPA templates

Every hackathon contributes back: winning prototypes get hardened and added to the library. **Each event leaves Lovable smarter.**

## The Hall of Fame — the public scale-flywheel

A public showcase (lovable.dev/hackathons) of every customer hackathon's standout build, with explicit credit to the customer team.

- **Customer benefit**: their team is publicly recognized; recruiting tool, internal pride, board-deck slide
- **Lovable benefit**: searchable proof at every prospect's first Google query
- **Compounding**: the Hall of Fame becomes the *de facto* enterprise vibe-coding case-study library — the moat against v0

## Where 1:Many turns into pipeline

Industry flagships (Format 4) don't sell direct — but they recruit:

1. **Future 1:1 customers** — exec attendees see live execution, request a private edition
2. **Future hires** — engineers and PMs in the room become next-quarter Lovable hires
3. **Future founders** — best builders found Lovable-built companies; Lovable's investment fund participates (the GoodOmen / Stardust pattern, repeated)
4. **Future advocates** — alumni publicly rep Lovable, become the volunteer evangelist layer

The flagship is the **community-creation lever**, not a sales lever. Treating it as a sales lever is the most common scale failure.

## Org-design implication

**1 hackathon producer per 5 active 1:1 customers.** Beyond that, the Applications group must exist or quality collapses.

In year 1: 1 producer (me), 1 Applications PM, 0–1 SE secondment.
In year 2: 3 producers (UK + DACH + Nordics), 2 Applications PMs, dedicated SE pool.
In year 3: regional model — each major market (UK, DACH, France, Nordics, US East, US West) has 1 producer + a shared Applications group.
