# Stakeholders

> A hackathon is the most cross-functional thing Lovable does. Misalignment kills it.

## Internal RACI — who owns what

| Workstream | R · Responsible | A · Accountable | C · Consulted | I · Informed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sourcing & qualifying** | AE / Strategic Initiatives | CRO | Marketing | CS |
| **Format design** | Strategic Initiatives | Strategic Initiatives | CS, SE | Marketing |
| **Problem-statement co-design** | SE + customer champion | Strategic Initiatives | CS | AE |
| **Run-of-show** | Strategic Initiatives | Strategic Initiatives | Marketing | All |
| **Marketing wrap (PR, content, social)** | Enterprise Marketing | CMO / CRO | Strategic Initiatives | All |
| **Customer success conversion** | CS | Head of CS | Strategic Initiatives, AE | CRO |
| **Pricing & commercial follow-through** | AE | CRO | CS, Strategic Initiatives | Founders |
| **Hackathon Hall of Fame** | Strategic Initiatives | CMO | Founders | All |

## External stakeholders

- **Customer exec sponsor** — the budget signature; needs to attend day-of, even if briefly. Their presence converts "innovation theatre" into "leadership endorsement."
- **Customer hackathon champion** — full-time mid-level operator; their KPI is a working POC by day 30.
- **Builder participants** — 8 to 80 depending on format. Mixed seniority is *good* — engineers, PMs, designers, ops people. Not just devs.
- **Judging panel** — internal customer execs + 1 Lovable founder/exec + 1 external partner (foundation-model lab or vertical specialist).
- **Vendor partners** — NVIDIA (compute + venue gravitas), foundation-model labs (Anthropic / OpenAI / Google) for credit stacks and judge presence, AWS / GCP / Azure for cloud credits.

## The cross-functional cadence

- **Pre (T-30 to T-0)** — weekly sync between Strategic Initiatives, AE, CS, and SE. Marketing joins from T-14.
- **Day-of** — Strategic Initiatives runs the floor. CS shadows winners for handoff prep. SE is the technical backstop.
- **Post (T+0 to T+30)** — daily standup for first week (CS + SE + customer champion); weekly thereafter until POC ships.

## Failure modes I've seen elsewhere

1. **Marketing owns the hackathon.** Treats it as content, not pipeline. Outcomes pretty, conversion low.
2. **Sales owns the hackathon.** Treats every booth as a demo opportunity. Builders bounce, no real prototypes built.
3. **CS owns the hackathon.** Treats it as a relationship-building exercise. Misses pipeline and brand entirely.

The right answer is **Strategic Initiatives owns the hackathon**, with marketing and CS as deeply-engaged partners. The hackathon's revenue contribution is the metric — everything else flows from that.
