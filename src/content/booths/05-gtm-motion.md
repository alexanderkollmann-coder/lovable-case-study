# GTM Motion

> The hackathon doesn't end at 18:00. The deal does.

## The post-hackathon funnel

```
17:30  ─  Hackathon → MOU (Optionsvereinbarung) signed by exec sponsor
T+1    ─  POC kickoff agenda sent to customer champion
T+7    ─  POC begins — 3 winning ideas, 1 lead per idea on customer side
T+30   ─  POC review with exec sponsor + Lovable Applied AI Architect
T+60   ─  SoW negotiation begins, 1 idea picks up a production budget
T+90   ─  SoW signed, contract booked
T+120  ─  Production deployment with hand-off to customer's internal team
```

This is **the most important booth in this entire presentation**, because the hackathon's value is *defined* by what happens between T+1 and T+90. A great hackathon with no follow-through is innovation theatre. A modest hackathon with disciplined follow-through is **revenue**.

## My role: Applied AI Architect

This is the role I see myself in across the GTM motion. I'm not a salesperson; I'm the technical-and-strategic owner of the customer's outcome from MOU through production.

| Phase | What I do |
|---|---|
| **Hackathon day** | Run the room (see Execution booth) |
| **Day +1 to +30: POC** | Embedded with customer champion. Architect the winning prototype into a production-shaped POC. Lovable Cloud, Supabase, governance primitives. 3–4 hrs/day with their team. |
| **Day +30 to +60: SoW** | Co-author the SoW with the customer's procurement, in-house counsel, and exec sponsor. Pre-cleared scope, deliverables, IP, support model. |
| **Day +60 to +90: Production hand-off** | Pair with Lovable's CS team to onboard customer engineers; deliver runbook + ops playbook. |
| **Ongoing** | Quarterly business reviews, sit on the customer's AI steering committee as an external Lovable representative. |

This is the **Forward-Deployed Engineer pattern** translated to enterprise GTM: deeply technical, customer-facing, architecturally responsible, commercially aware. Palantir built FDE as a wedge into government; Anthropic and OpenAI now run the same play. Lovable should too — and Strategic Initiatives is where it lives.

## Post-event RACI

The internal RACI shifts at 17:30. Hackathon-day owners hand off; Day-2 owners pick up.

| Workstream | Day 1 owner | Day 2+ owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| MOU drafting & signing | Strategic Initiatives | (signed — closed) | Pre-cleared template; legal lives at T-30 |
| POC delivery | Strategic Initiatives | **Applied AI Architect** + Customer Success | Daily standup for first week, weekly thereafter |
| Customer relationship | Customer exec sponsor + AE | **AE** | AE owns the deal commercially; AAA owns the outcome |
| Procurement / SoW | Strategic Initiatives | **AE** + Lovable Legal | AAA writes scope; AE negotiates terms |
| Production handoff | (none day-of) | **Customer Success** + Solutions Engineer | AAA stays consulted |
| Hackathon learnings → flywheel | Strategic Initiatives | **Strategic Initiatives** | Feeds into Scale booth |

## Why hackathon → POC conversion is so high

Industry-typical conversion rates for paid POCs from cold inbound: ~10–15%. Hackathon-sourced POCs convert at **65%+**, because:

1. **The customer team has already shipped working software.** The risk of "can it be done?" is already gone.
2. **The exec sponsor has already publicly committed.** They signed the MOU in front of their own team. Backing out has political cost.
3. **The technical buyer has already passed procurement.** SOC 2, audit, data residency — all answered before the hackathon. POC starts on Day 1, not Day 30.
4. **Three problem statements, three POC paths.** The customer picks one, but if the first stalls, the other two are warm fallbacks.

## The procurement-acceleration pack

Every hackathon ships with a pre-signed addendum bundle:

- Pre-filled SIG/CAIQ security questionnaire
- Standardised DPA + DPIA starter template
- Penetration-test summary (current within 90 days)
- Architectural diagrams with data residency annotations
- Model-training opt-out language
- Sample SoW with placeholder scope brackets

Customer's procurement team can mark up the bundle on Day 1 of the POC instead of starting from zero on Day 30. This is the single biggest cycle-time saver in the entire motion.

## Where deals die — and how I prevent it

| Failure mode | Frequency | My intervention |
|---|---|---|
| **Exec sponsor moves on** before SoW | Common | I send a personal handover note + intro to successor in week 6 |
| **POC champion leaves the company** | Occasional | The MOU names *two* champions — primary + alternate |
| **Procurement re-runs from zero** at SoW stage | Common | Procurement-acceleration pack is signed at MOU stage, not SoW stage |
| **SoW scope creeps beyond original problem** | Common | Locked scope template; expansion goes into Statement of Work v2, not v1 |
| **Customer engineers can't operate the prototype** | Sometimes | Production hand-off is a *named deliverable* in the SoW, not an afterthought |
